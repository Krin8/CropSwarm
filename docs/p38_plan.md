# P38 Kansas-to-Oklahoma transfer diagnosis preregistration

Commit before evaluating. Use cached P25 Kansas training features, P32 Oklahoma features and the exact saved P32 `20_to_40` predictions for ridge, random forest and the two Oklahoma own-state district-held-out baselines. Use all preferred-with-fallback county-years in both states, as in that transfer case. No fitting, model or parameter changes, new data, or network. Join by county FIPS and harvest year, checking uniqueness and identical observed yields and practice labels.

## Feature distribution and overlap

- Use all five P25 features in their original units. For both states report minimum, 5th percentile, median, 95th percentile and maximum by feature. Use linear empirical quantiles. From Kansas training rows only, define two inclusive marginal ranges for each feature: full minimum–maximum and central 5th–95th percentile. Report the number and percentage of Oklahoma rows below and above each range, per feature, and outside at least one range overall. Count each row once in the overall union. Report Kansas's own overall retention at the central cutoffs for context.
- Define the overlap restriction as Oklahoma rows inside the inclusive Kansas 5th–95th percentile band on all five features. This axis-aligned rule is a marginal screening proxy and need not establish joint support. Use the full cohort, the overlap subset and its complement. On each identical set of rows compare both fixed Kansas predictions and both saved Oklahoma baselines in RMSE, bu/acre. Also report paired RMSE differences versus each baseline with descriptive 95% percentile intervals, using the P25/P27 seed, 1,000 paired whole USDA district draws and existing quantiles. A subset with fewer than two district blocks has no interval. Do not change the cutoffs after viewing results.

## Error location and reporting

- For each Kansas learner, group Oklahoma errors by harvest year, USDA reporting district and each feature's below / inside / above central Kansas band. Report row count, mean signed error (prediction minus observed yield), RMSE, and share of all squared error. Feature groups partition rows separately within each feature. Show counts and squared-error shares so a large group can be distinguished from a high-error group. Empty bins are reported explicitly.
- Define a few groups in advance as the three years or two districts with the largest squared-error totals per learner, with ties resolved by chronological year or ascending district code. Report their combined share of all squared error and their combined share of test rows. Call errors concentrated in a few groups if they account for more than half of total squared error. Report the concentration shares even if they fall below that threshold.
- Describe NASS reporting mix on the exact Kansas training and Oklahoma evaluation cohorts as counts and shares of preferred non-irrigated and all-practice fallback rows. These labels summarize reporting. They do not identify actual irrigation in all-practice records.
- Report every feature, subgroup, and overlap comparison, including evidence that does not explain the gap. Treat covariate shift, missed year or district effects, and management or reporting differences as hypotheses. Same-row baseline comparisons and filtered performance do not establish causation. County weather is a gridded centroid proxy and county-average yield associations do not validate rotation recommendations. All results are illustrative and need local validation.
