# P31 India climate–yield preregistration

This plan fixes the study before inspecting the archive for this task, fetching weather or evaluating any India models. The question is whether the P25/P27 weather-feature recipe improves held-out estimates of historical yields in another region. The comparison describes weather–yield associations in aggregate data. Crop rotation quality is outside this study.

## Archive, crop and geography

- Use the same [Maiti et al. study archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10958118) used in P20. Inspect its documentation and column definitions for all explicitly kharif yield series. List the candidate crops, seasonal coverage, district coverage and irrigation evidence. An annual or mixed-season series is ineligible unless the archive explicitly documents it as kharif.
- An eligible crop needs at least 20 unique finite positive yield years in each of at least 50 districts with valid archival geography. Use the intersection of available crop-year records with the P25/P27 1990–2024 harvest-year envelope. Count a district-year once. Keep exact duplicate rows once and exclude district-years whose valid yields conflict. Record exclusions by district and year.
- Among eligible crops, select the one with the lowest irrigated share if the archive provides comparable crop-specific irrigated and total area. Use pooled irrigated area divided by pooled total area over district-years with documented units and paired coverage. Record the paired coverage. Compare only shares with available, comparable denominators. If comparable shares are unavailable, require an archive-documented rainfed-dominant crop. Use alphabetical archive crop name to break exact ties among eligible candidates with equivalent evidence. If the archive cannot establish a qualifying crop under either route, stop before weather requests and record which condition failed. Do not infer irrigation from a crop name or guess at undocumented fields. Keep the selected crop fixed after screening and document any later loss of eligibility.
- Use the archive's fixed historical district polygons and identifier mapping. Use the archive's published centroid coordinates if available, otherwise calculate geometry centroids from those same public polygons in an equal-area projection and record CRS, algorithm, coordinates and the archive hash. Use the historical state grouping encoded by the archive. Keep distinct archival identifiers distinct. Do not map to current districts or reconstruct split observations from names. Disclose any district boundary or irrigation ambiguity the archive leaves unresolved. Do not fetch geography from another service.
- Retain districts with at least 20 complete matched crop-weather years, and require at least 50 remaining districts and at least four state groups. Four states leave at least three training states for the unchanged grouped inner split. A district failing the threshold is excluded and listed with its reason. Do not lower these gates or choose a substitute crop after inspecting weather coverage.

## Fixed weather, features and evaluation

- Use cached NASA POWER AG daily point requests in local solar time with the P25 fields and requested date chunks. Resume at the district level, with the existing bounded retry policy. Reject incompatible units and flag missing days or fill values, including `-999`. Do not substitute sources or impute weather.
- Reuse the P25 seasonal feature builder, fixed bucket storage proxy, zero supplied irrigation and no MODIS features. Apply the existing CropSwarm kharif/monsoon window convention and the selected crop's unchanged catalog duration, water demand and daily heat threshold. Fix the window from the existing P20 Telangana monsoon onset and apply it consistently, with a stated transfer caveat. Do not use yield to choose window timing, water inputs or heat thresholds. If the selected crop lacks a catalog match or the needed values, stop rather than replace it.
- Retain the source yield units and label RMSE explicitly. The weather features are seasonal rainfall, modeled water deficit, heat days, mean solar radiation and mean air temperature. Apply the unchanged P25 mean and trend baselines per district, with the equal-district training fallback for an unseen district. Apply ridge with scaling and random forest to training-fold yield anomalies and add back the applicable trend. Reuse the P25 code and configuration. The P25 internal identifier and outcome field names can be adapted at the India boundary without changing the math or Kansas files.
- Evaluate once on the final eligible data. Use leave-one-harvest-year-out and leave-one-archival-state-out with the same three-fold grouped inner selection. Reuse the exact P25/P27 settings, seeds, model grids, RMSE selection and block bootstrap. Report both baselines, both learned models, every tried configuration, all outer folds and sample sizes, and RMSE, R² and Spearman with descriptive intervals. Report paired RMSE differences against the trend baseline. No India hyperparameter changes, post-hoc feature choices or alternate splits after viewing results. Use synthetic fixtures for development checks.
- Compare Kansas-trained models on India only if crop identity and the season window align with Kansas winter wheat, including season timing and crop-parameter comparability. Otherwise record the reason for skipping and make no transfer claim.
- Network access is limited to retrieval from the P20 archive and cacheable NASA POWER requests. Log archive members, hashes, source metadata and omissions. Keep all P25/P27 and farm outputs unchanged. Report any failed prerequisite and the missing evidence before making a numerical claim.
