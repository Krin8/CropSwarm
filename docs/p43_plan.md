# P43 literature-informed scoring variant preregistration

Use committed CropSwarm inputs and publications already opened and read in P35. This is an illustrative what-if needing local validation. The existing scorer, settings, published rankings and saved outputs are read-only. All three changes apply only to the Kansas reference farm. Telangana and Makueni have no benchmark for these changes, so the original scoring applies there. Use the original farm labels and all four unchanged presets.

## Fixed corrections

1. **Nitrogen (N).** Replace the soybean predecessor credit with 0 kg N/ha when followed by winter wheat and rounded 45 kg N/ha when followed by grain maize, grain sorghum or sunflower. Those crops have warm-season recommendations in the source and are represented in the catalog. Retain the existing credits for other pairs. The original whole-catalog normalization bounds apply to every variant, including those derived from each P22 draw. This isolates the transition effect. Evidence grade for the conditional rates is literature. The farm-level transfer and applicability without a soil test remain assumptions. [K-State MF2586, cool- and warm-season Previous Crop Adjustment tables](https://www.brown.k-state.edu/crops-livestock/documents/Soiltestinterpretationsandfertilizerrecommenations.pdf)
2. **Water carryover (W).** Apply only to a Kansas winter-wheat slot immediately after a legume. Use 43, 70 and 97 mm reductions in planting-season plant-available water, with 70 mm the midpoint. The source's 0–1.8 m reference is 201 mm after fallow and 104–158 mm after sole legumes. Do not subtract this deeper-profile measurement from the engine's 0–30 cm storage. Keep the original shallow bucket and use a separate, one-time planting reserve for this diagnostic. Compute the additional unmet demand from reducing the reserve by comparing two otherwise identical counterfactual windows. For each year with before-irrigation bucket shortfall D, the added shortfall is max(0, D − (201 − reduction)) − max(0, D − 201), then multiply by 1 minus irrigation supply fraction. This is equivalent to using reserve water only to meet shortfalls until exhausted. Add only this difference to the original slot deficit. Cap the corrected unmet amount at the crop's unmet-demand ceiling, crop demand × (1 minus irrigation supply fraction), for that year. Compute the mean normalized water score and reapply the original median unmet-fraction feasibility threshold to that transition. All other crop pairs keep their existing water scores and feasibility. Grade the source values literature and the reserve translation and transfer from the trial's legumes to the catalog's legumes assumption, validate locally. This is a comparison of starting-water effects in the score. It is neither a soil-state reconstruction nor a daily deep-root water balance. [Holman et al., Table 5, Garden City](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2135/cropsci2017.05.0324) · [FAO-56 Chapter 8, initial depletion and Equation 85](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm)
3. **Disease (D).** Treat grain sorghum as the only newly recognized break crop for wheat tan spot in Kansas. Use the four sampling dates in the matched 1991 no-till trial. Compare wheat after sorghum with continuous wheat and take the median proportional reduction, median of 1 − rotated severity / continuous severity. Scale the applied same-family transition penalty by the remaining fraction. This stays within the reported date-specific effect range. Retain the continuous-wheat comparator and the original bare-slot weight, and leave other pairs as scored before. Source evidence supports reduced severity for this sequence and does not establish that sorghum is universally a non-host or that the composite risk index measures tan spot. Grade the measured reduction literature and the conversion to penalty units assumption, validate locally. [Bockus and Claassen, Tables 2–3, pp. 634–635](https://www.apsnet.org/publications/PlantDisease/BackIssues/Documents/1992Articles/PlantDisease76n06_633.PDF)

## Fixed comparisons

- Run the original plus every combination of N, W and D. Wherever W is on, run all three water reductions. This gives 16 configurations including the original, for every farm and preset. Evaluate nominal exact searches at each farm's existing horizon. Enumerate whole-year-canonical eligible alternatives using the existing season, adjacent-repeat and water constraints. Reapply the original water threshold when the W transition changes the median. Keep baseline and the published shortlist as fixed reference rows and report their metrics and flags even when a row is ineligible. Compare the nominal winners among feasible alternatives without the baseline. Require the reconstructed original feasible counts, best scores and reference metrics to match the saved nominal artifacts before comparing. Exactly equal objectives use the first canonical row.
- For every farm/preset/variant, report both winner identities and objective scores, whether the original winner is still optimal within the P16c numerical tolerance, Spearman over all common feasible alternatives (average ranks for tied values), the common count and any added or excluded cycles. Count exact ties and candidates within 0.01 weighted-score points of the winner. Report top-two gaps and whether a near-tie appeared or disappeared. Report how many canonical cycles entered or left the near-top set. The 0.01 tolerance is the existing design choice used in P16/P22. Use the same weights and definitions everywhere. Compare all six raw and normalized metrics for the published baseline and shortlist.
- Recreate the Kansas P22 fixed-seed draw stream using its input inventory and exact builder. Run all 512 draws, with the same altered weather windows, farmer inputs, constraints and weights, and verify the original winners and change fractions against the saved P22 study. Within each draw P22 allows the baseline to take precedence on a tie, even when it has flags. Report each variant's label-change rate relative to its own nominal winner under that same convention. Also report changes exceeding P22's 0.01 tolerance or excluding the reference, and paired variant-versus-original winner disagreement on identical draws. Outside Kansas the variant is an identity on every draw, so reuse the saved P22 rates exactly. Use matched draws and no new tuning. No confidence interval is assigned to these design-range frequencies.

All results describe how three assumptions change a decision aid. They are not field estimates of nitrogen, uptake, soil water or pathogen severity and do not establish rotation quality.
