# P35 literature cross-check of scoring

The comparison covers four components of the illustrative scoring model. Published values apply to specific crops, management systems and locations. Every model value and scenario remains illustrative and needs local validation. An **inside range** finding means the listed comparison contains the model value. An **outside range** finding applies to the named reference only. **No usable source** means a comparable numerical bound was missing or the reference measures a different quantity. For disease-family labels, “inside range” records a categorical taxonomic match only.

## Formula and parameter inventory

The [crop catalog](../data/crops.csv), [scoring code](../src/scoring.py) and [scoring policy](../config/scoring.json) determine the following values.

- **Nitrogen.** For every slot, subtract current `n_demand_kg_ha` from the preceding crop’s `n_credit_kg_ha`. The first slot receives credit from the last slot of the repeated cycle. Average in kg N/ha per slot and scale linearly between minus the largest catalog demand and the largest catalog credit. There is no soil-N state or second-year credit. `is_legume` is descriptive here. The score uses the numeric credit regardless of that flag. The resulting balance is a comparison proxy. Fertilizer rates and profile nitrate are different variables. Montana’s Tables 6, 9 and 14 describe moisture, soil and yield conditions that the fixed score excludes. Minnesota’s corn tables also adjust for the previous crop and management. [Montana State Extension, Fertilizer Guidelines](https://www.montana.edu/extension/Full_HTML_Pubs/eb161-fertilizerguidelines/EB0161-FertilizerGuidelinesForMontanaCrops.html) · [Minnesota Extension, Fertilizing corn, Tables 1–3 and profile nitrate](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/nutrient-management-for-minnesota-crops/fertilizing-corn-in-minnesota)
- **Water use.** Divide `water_need_mm` equally over `season_length_days`. The farm season calendar supplies the onset using `seasons_allowed`. Each window starts with empty storage. For each day, add NASA POWER corrected rainfall to stored water, subtract daily demand, accumulate any unmet amount, and retain surplus up to the site’s 0–30 cm storage value. Multiply total unmet water by one minus the farm’s assumed irrigation fraction. The score is one minus mean unmet water divided by demand per slot, with an unplanted slot given zero demand and zero unmet fraction. FAO-56 varies daily demand with reference ET and crop stage, and initializes root-zone depletion separately. A catalog ET total therefore cannot establish the model’s within-season allocation, irrigation coverage or deficit. The paddy ET comparison also excludes water used in saturation, water-layer establishment and percolation or seepage of 4–8 mm/day in the FAO example. [FAO-56 Chapter 6, Equation 58 and Table 12](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0b.htm) · [FAO-56 Chapter 8, Equations 85–88](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm) · [FAO irrigation training manual, Chapter 4 §4.4](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e08.htm)
- **Erosion cover.** The raw proxy per window is mean site slope from SRTM × peak daily corrected rainfall × (1 − `cover_score`). The bare slot uses the full reference window and lacks the cover reduction. Average over windows and slots. The score is one minus the raw proxy divided by site slope × maximum daily rain in the site record, with a score of one at zero slope. The formula has no soil erodibility or separate terms for residue from the previous crop, canopy, roughness or management. Published ranges of surface cover and calibrated RUSLE subfactors cannot be transferred directly to fixed per-crop index values. [USDA NRCS, RUSLE Chapter 4](https://efotg.sc.egov.usda.gov/references/Agency/OK/RUSLE_Chap4_C_Factors.pdf) · [FAO Kenya assessment §4.4.3](https://www.fao.org/4/t0733e/T0733E05.htm)
- **Disease-family risk.** Subtract 0.5 × the fraction of cyclic adjacent slots with active crops in the same `disease_family` and 0.5 × the share of bare slots. A zero-day slot defines bare. The 0.5 weights are design choices. The index uses immediate neighbors and a botanical family lookup. Disease-specific hosts, duration of a break, residues and resistant varieties can change the actual direction. Published severity and incidence do not share units with this index. [Saskatchewan, Principles and Practices of Crop Rotation, Table 9](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/76296/formats/85517/download)

All final sub-scores are clipped to 0–1. The model’s `root_depth_class` is descriptive for this scorer. The rainfall and temperature thresholds are climate-fit inputs. The policy also excludes alternatives above a median unmet-water share of 0.8 and permits a 45-day calendar extension in its suitability check. These are design choices. The irrigation fraction and topsoil storage are site inputs with their own assumptions and source records. Crop-window duration comparisons below describe growth-cycle examples. They cannot identify local planting windows. [Farm configuration](../config/farms.json) · [Kansas calendar](../data/processed/seasons_us_high_plains_b.json)

## What the numerical comparisons show

- **Nitrogen after legumes depends on the successor.** The catalog adds **22 kg N/ha** for common bean or field pea and **34 kg N/ha** for soybean to the next slot before subtracting current demand. The first-year Minnesota corn references are **20 and 30 lb N/acre** respectively, about **22 and 34 kg N/ha** at catalog precision. For maize after common bean the model moves from −120 to −98 kg N/ha in the maize slot. In a two-slot cycle, this isolated change is +11 kg N/ha per slot before changing any demand for the replacement crop. The model's +34 for soybean before winter wheat exceeds K-State’s **0** for that transition. The same model credit falls below K-State’s **40 lb N/acre** for soybean before warm-season crops. Pigeonpea’s +30 is below the **38–49 kg N/ha** maize fertilizer-equivalent values from one Hyderabad trial. Chickpea’s +25 is below the **40–114.6 kg N/ha** range for wheat in the six estimable years of a Queensland trial. The highest estimate followed frost damage to chickpea. Both experiments agree with a positive model direction. The magnitudes use different crop responses and local treatment contexts. [Minnesota Extension, Table 1](https://extension.umn.edu/natural-resources/conservation/agricultural-soil-and-water/soil-biology) · [K-State MF2586, Previous Crop Adjustment tables](https://www.brown.k-state.edu/crops-livestock/documents/Soiltestinterpretationsandfertilizerrecommenations.pdf) · [Kumar Rao et al., Figure 3](https://oar.icrisat.org/7864/1/Expl%20Agric._19_131-141_1983.pdf) · [Dalal et al., Table 6](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/11227/1/Sustaining%20productivity%20of%20a%20Vertisol%20at%20Warra%2C%20Queensland%2C%20with%20fertilisers%2C%20no-tillage%2C%20or%20legumes.%205.%20Wheat%20yields%2C%20nitrogen%20benefits%20and%20water-use%20efficiency%20of%20chickpea-wheat%20rotation.pdf)
- **Water demand and carryover.** Sorghum’s **450 mm** is inside the broad FAO sorghum/millet interval and above the **254–381 mm** Texas A&M early-group estimate whose maturity range includes the model’s 105 days. Both are context-dependent. The water balance also gives the same starting water to every following crop. In a Garden City trial, wheat started with **201 mm** in the 0–1.8 m profile after fallow and **104–158 mm** after the tested sole legumes, a drop of **43–97 mm**. The observed direction is absent from the model. Replacing a 0–30 cm capacity number with those deeper-profile differences would conflate two quantities. For cereal rye, the Iowa lysimeter paper reports May ET of **1.9–3.1 mm/day** and bare evaporation of **1.0–2.0 mm/day**. It supports greater water use in those months and does not establish the model’s **220 mm** over its 100-day period. [FAO Table 14](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e07.htm) · [Texas A&M Table 9](https://ccag.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2020/12/PUB_Irrigated-and-Dryland-Grain-Sorghum-Production.pdf) · [Holman et al., Table 5](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2135/cropsci2017.05.0324) · [Qi and Helmers, Table 3](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.2136/vzj2008.0163)
- **Erosion direction and limits.** At equal slope, rainfall and exposure, rye’s 0.85 cover index implies an **85%** lower raw proxy than bare soil. A Western European cover-crop synthesis estimated **72%** less soil loss on average and **8–89%** across modeled control-tillage categories. The model effect falls inside that broad comparison, which includes multiple species and managements. The score also assigns **zero** change to the same winter-wheat window when pea replaces fallow beforehand. The Palouse trial’s RUSLE surface-cover subfactor was **0.57** in conventional fallow–wheat and **0.073** in no-till pea–wheat, with both crop sequence and tillage changing. That contrast cannot establish a pea-only calibration. [Clément et al., Figures 2 and 3b](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/316282/1/Soil%20Use%20and%20Management%20-%202024%20-%20Clement%20-%20How%20much%20do%20conservation%20cropping%20practices%20mitigate%20runoff%20and%20soil%20erosion.pdf) · [Langdale et al., Table 8](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/30200525/91--%20Cover%20Crop%20Effects%20on%20Soil%20Erosion.pdf)
- **Disease direction.** Switching from wheat after wheat to wheat after sorghum gives **zero** reduction in the model’s transition penalty because both belong to Poaceae. In the Kansas no-till trial’s 1991 observations, wheat after sorghum reduced tan-spot-affected leaf area by **13.63–42.45 percentage points** over four measurement dates compared with no-till continuous wheat. Early disease still occurred in the rotation in 1990. The 0.5 score penalty cannot be judged against a leaf-area percentage. The direction differs for that crop pair. [Bockus and Claassen, Tables 2–3 and discussion](https://www.apsnet.org/publications/PlantDisease/BackIssues/Documents/1992Articles/PlantDisease76n06_633.PDF)

The publisher’s APS paper on Iowa gray leaf spot and tar spot, the ScienceDirect Córdoba chickpea paper, the MDPI publisher page for the Bankina wheat leaf-disease study, and the K-State Pressbooks USLE chapter were inaccessible in this check. No finding relies on those pages. The Patil Table 1 and Wani Table 10.7 PDFs were readable from indexed full-text copies after direct-open errors and were used only for their stated rows.

## Read-only what-if

The scenario substitutes each reported endpoint for the catalog’s chickpea credit, pigeonpea credit or early sorghum water demand in memory, one change at a time, using all three demo farms. Those three inputs derive from regional or crop-pair comparisons. Applying them as uniform catalog values is a sensitivity test. The nitrogen bounds are recomputed when catalog credits change, which can change normalized scores even when the affected crop is absent.

The Kansas-specific scenarios substitute the rounded K-State amounts only at the named predecessor–successor pairs, including a conditional fallow credit when there is no profile test. They use the original nitrogen normalization. A separate direction-only trial sets the sorghum → wheat family penalty to zero to test whether recognizing a break would affect nominal rankings. The zero is a design choice, not a disease estimate. The structural carryover gaps have no defensible one-number equivalent and are excluded from the numeric substitutions.

Every scenario reruns exact canonical search at the published horizon for the four presets and rescores the baseline and the frozen displayed shortlist. Nominal reconstruction matches the saved feasible counts, optima and metric scores. The comparison uses an absolute tolerance of 1e-10 and the first canonical sequence within that tolerance for representative exact winners. The fixed-shortlist ranking rounds weighted scores to ten decimal places and breaks ties by saved display order. Sensitivity, uncertainty, ACO verification and robustness outputs use the affected score terms and would need separate runs after any adopted change. P15 and P20 also use sorghum demand when computing sorghum stress in Kansas. P19 and the county winter-wheat learners do not use that input. Only the nominal comparison was rerun here. The [scenario artifact](../data/processed/literature_what_if_p35.json) records exact alternatives, scores and ties, feasibility counts, all six raw and normalized metric deltas and fixed-shortlist ranks. It is kept separate from the app’s published results.

Across 24 farm–scenario cases and four presets, the nominal exact winner ceased to be optimal in 11 comparisons. Feasible counts were unchanged in these tests. In Kansas, the sorghum endpoints changed baseline unmet water by -49.68 and -20.09 mm per slot. The conditional fallow credit changed baseline modeled nitrogen by +7.33 kg N/ha per slot. Pigeonpea endpoints changed raw nitrogen for all four saved Telangana alternatives, without displacing their nominal exact winner for any preset. A fixed-shortlist order change can occur independently of an exact optimum change.
